## 2. Hypothesis Testing Results

### Hypothesis 1: The Core Problem
**Statement:** Beginners want to crochet, but fail because sourcing materials and reading written patterns is overwhelmingly frustrating.
* **Target Metric:** 7/10 users must agree this is their primary blocker.
* **Actual Results:** 4/5 (80%) 
* **Status:** ✅ **PASS**
* **Key Insights Learned:** 
  * Users are not opposed to spending money; they are opposed to looking stupid in a craft store.
  * 3 out of 5 explicitly mentioned abandoning a YouTube tutorial because they didn't know what "yarn weight" meant.

### Hypothesis 2: The Alternative / Competition
**Statement:** Watching free YouTube tutorials and buying cheap yarn is not a "good enough" solution; they will pay a premium for convenience.
* **Target Metric:** 6/10 users must choose a KES 2,500 kit over a KES 1,000 DIY route.
* **Actual Results:** 2/5 (40%) 
* **Status:** ❌ **FAIL**
* **Key Insights Learned:** 
  * KES 2,500 is perceived as too high of a risk for something they "might be terrible at." 
  * They want convenience, but their willingness to pay for a *first-time* trial is lower than expected.

## 3. Executive Summary & Pivot Strategy

### Summary of Findings
The interviews confirmed strong Problem-Fit regarding the friction of starting a new hobby. The target demographic (young, educated, full-time employed) deeply feels the pain of sourcing materials and deciphering complex patterns. However, our pricing assumption failed the market test. The target audience views KES 2,500 as too high of a financial risk for a trial hobby.

### Pivot Hypotheses (Based on Failed Metrics)
* **Failed Assumption:** Beginners will pay KES 2,500 for a curated premium kit.
* **Pivot Hypothesis A (Product Pivot):** Beginners will pay KES 1,200 for a "Mini Starter Kit" (e.g., a simple coaster or keychain) to test their skills before upgrading to the KES 2,500 premium bag kit.
* **Pivot Hypothesis B (Business Model Pivot):** Beginners will pay KES 2,500 if the kit includes a "100% Money-Back Guarantee if you can't finish it," removing the financial risk of failure.
