# Craft Atelier: Lean Canvas Models

## Model 1: The "Craft-Curious" Beginner (B2C)
| 1. Problem | 2. Solution | 3. Unique Value Proposition | 4. Unfair Advantage | 5. Customer Segments |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| - Sourcing craft materials is overwhelming.<br>- Written patterns are too difficult to read.<br>- High fear of failing the project.<br>- Want a screen-free, tactile hobby. | - All-in-one curated kits (yarn, hooks, guides).<br>- Interactive digital step-by-step videos.<br>- Fast, trackable Boda Boda delivery. | **"Everything you need to crochet your first masterpiece, delivered in one box with idiot-proof video guides."** | - Proprietary fulfillment engine ensuring 2-hour local delivery.<br>- High-quality local yarn sourcing network. | Urban Millennials & Gen Z seeking mindful, screen-free hobbies. |
| **8. Key Metrics** | **9. Channels** |
| - Kit completion rate<br>- Repeat purchase rate<br>- Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) | - Instagram / TikTok Ads<br>- Influencer crafting challenges<br>- Organic SEO |
| **7. Cost Structure** | **6. Revenue Streams** |
| - Raw materials (Yarn, Hooks, Packaging)<br>- Boda Boda delivery subsidies<br>- Marketing & Ad spend<br>- Warehouse labor (Packers) | - Direct single-kit sales (KES 1,500 - 4,500)<br>- "Kit of the Month" subscription billing |

## Model 2: The Corporate / Event Buyer (B2B)
| 1. Problem | 2. Solution | 3. Unique Value Proposition | 4. Unfair Advantage | 5. Customer Segments |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| - Traditional team-building activities are stale.<br>- Need tangible "wellness" gifts for employees.<br>- Hard to coordinate gifts for remote teams. | - B2B Wholesale Portal.<br>- Bulk automated ordering and invoicing.<br>- Custom branded boxes. | **"A mindful, hands-on wellness experience delivered directly to your employees' desks."** | - Scalable warehouse backend that handles bulk fulfillment without disrupting B2C operations. | HR Managers, Event Coordinators, Corporate Wellness Officers. |
| **8. Key Metrics** | **9. Channels** |

| **7. Cost Structure** | **6. Revenue Streams** |
| - Bulk material procurement<br>- Custom branding/printing costs<br>- B2B Sales commissions | - Bulk volume sales (50+ units)<br>- Custom corporate branding fees |

## Model 3: The Independent Designer (Creator Economy)
| 1. Problem | 2. Solution | 3. Unique Value Proposition | 4. Unfair Advantage | 5. Customer Segments |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| - Designers can only monetize digital PDF patterns for $5.<br>- Cannot afford to manufacture physical kits.<br>- No access to supply chain or delivery fleet. | - Creator Portal for pattern submission.<br>- Automated royalty splits.<br>- Craft Atelier handles all physical ops. | **"Turn your crochet pattern into a physical retail product and earn passive royalties without packing a single box."** | - Existing bespoke logistics and packing software that standardizes external designs instantly. | Local crochet artists, Instagram crafting influencers. |
| **8. Key Metrics** | **9. Channels** |
| - Number of Creator Patterns submitted<br>- Total royalty payouts per month<br>- Creator retention rate | - Instagram DMs to niche artists<br>- Crafting community forums<br>- Word of mouth |
| **7. Cost Structure** | **6. Revenue Streams** |
| - Royalty payouts to designers (e.g., 15% cut)<br>- Prototyping new creator kits | - 85% revenue share on all Creator Kit sales.<br>- Free marketing generated by Creators promoting their own kits. |
