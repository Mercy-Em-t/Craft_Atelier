# Problem-Solution Fit: Falsifiable Tests & Validation Questions

To prevent us from building features based on false hope, every hypothesis below has been engineered to be **falsifiable**. If the Pass/Fail metric is not met during our user interviews, the hypothesis is proven false, and we must pivot our strategy.

---

## Segment 1: The "Craft-Curious" Beginner (B2C)

### 1. The Core Problem Hypothesis
**Hypothesis:** Beginners want to crochet, but fail because sourcing materials and reading written patterns is overwhelmingly frustrating.
**Falsifiable Test:** Interview 10 young professionals who have expressed a desire for a screen-free hobby.
* **PASS Metric:** At least 7 out of 10 must explicitly identify "not knowing what supplies to buy" or "patterns being too confusing" as their primary blocker to starting. (If fewer than 7 agree, our problem isn't painful enough).
* **Problem Fit Questions:**
  * *"Have you ever thought about taking up a physical craft? If yes, what exactly stopped you from starting?"*
  * *"When you tried to start a DIY project in the past, what was the most frustrating part of the setup?"*

### 2. The Competition/Alternative Hypothesis
**Hypothesis:** Watching free YouTube tutorials and buying cheap yarn from the local market is not a "good enough" solution; they will pay a premium for convenience.
**Falsifiable Test:** Pitch the curated Craft Atelier kit vs. the DIY approach to 10 interested beginners.
* **PASS Metric:** At least 6 out of 10 must choose the KES 2,500 curated kit over spending KES 1,000 to gather their own supplies.
* **Problem Fit Questions:**
  * *"What is the biggest drawback of using free YouTube tutorials to learn a craft?"*
  * *"Would you rather spend KES 1,000 gathering your own supplies across town over 3 days, or KES 2,500 to have everything perfectly curated and delivered in 2 hours? Why?"*

## Segment 2: The Corporate / Event Buyer (B2B)

### 1. The Core Problem Hypothesis
**Hypothesis:** HR Managers view traditional digital team-building (Zoom trivia) as stale, and coordinating physical shipping of wellness gifts to remote employees is a logistical nightmare.
**Falsifiable Test:** Interview 10 HR or Corporate Culture Managers.
* **PASS Metric:** At least 7 out of 10 must rate the logistics of coordinating remote employee gifts as a "High" or "Severe" pain point (4 or 5 out of 5).
* **Problem Fit Questions:**
  * *"What is the biggest complaint you hear from employees regarding virtual team-building events?"*
  * *"On a scale of 1 to 5, how painful is it to manage addresses and shipping logistics when sending physical gifts to a remote team?"*

### 2. The Competition/Alternative Hypothesis
**Hypothesis:** Existing corporate gifts (snack boxes, branded mugs) do not provide the mental-health-boosting experience companies are currently prioritizing.
**Falsifiable Test:** Ask 10 HR managers to compare a traditional corporate gift to an interactive craft kit for Wellness Month.
* **PASS Metric:** At least 6 out of 10 must prefer the interactive craft kit over the traditional alternative.
* **Problem Fit Questions:**
  * *"What was the last physical gift you sent to your team, and what was the feedback?"*
  * *"If you had a $30/head budget, would you rather send a premium snack box or a guided crafting kit for Mental Health Awareness month? Why?"*

## Segment 3: The Independent Designer (Creator Economy)

### 1. The Core Problem Hypothesis
**Hypothesis:** Crochet designers desperately want to sell physical kits to increase their revenue, but are completely blocked by the upfront capital required for inventory and logistics.
**Falsifiable Test:** Interview 10 Instagram crochet pattern designers with 10k+ followers.
* **PASS Metric:** At least 8 out of 10 must state they *want* to sell physical kits, and specifically cite logistics, time, or capital as the reason they currently do not.
* **Problem Fit Questions:**
  * *"Have you ever considered selling physical DIY kits of your designs? What prevented you from doing it?"*
  * *"How much time do you currently spend per week doing admin work or packing boxes, compared to actually designing?"*

### 2. The Competition/Alternative Hypothesis
**Hypothesis:** Designers will happily surrender the majority of the physical revenue (e.g., an 85% cut) in exchange for a "done-for-you" manufacturing and fulfillment service.
**Falsifiable Test:** Pitch the 15% passive royalty model to 10 designers.
* **PASS Metric:** At least 5 out of 10 designers must verbally agree that they would sign a contract for this model tomorrow. (If they say "I'd rather pack it myself and keep 100%", this model fails).
* **Problem Fit Questions:**
  * *"Are you satisfied with the income you generate purely from digital pattern sales on Etsy?"*
  * *"If a partner offered to manufacture, pack, and ship your physical kits for a 15% royalty cut where you do zero physical work, would you take it, or would you prefer to pack them yourself to keep a higher margin?"*
